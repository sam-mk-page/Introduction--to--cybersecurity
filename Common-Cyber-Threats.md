# Common Cyber Threats

## 1. Definitions

- **Threat**  
  A potential danger or circumstance that could exploit a vulnerability to cause harm to a system, network, or data.  
  *Example: A hacker trying to steal user data.*

- **Vulnerability**  
  A weakness or flaw in a system, application, or human behavior that can be exploited by a threat.  
  *Example: Outdated software with unpatched security bugs.*

- **Attack**  
  The actual exploitation of a vulnerability by a threat to compromise, damage, or gain unauthorized access to a system.  
  *Example: Running malware on a computer to steal files.*

---

## 2. Common Cyber Threats Explained + Examples + Protection

### 1. Phishing
**Explanation:**  
Phishing is a type of social engineering attack where attackers send fake emails, messages, or websites that look real to trick people into giving up passwords, card details, or login info.

**Real-life example:**  
A fake email from "Safaricom" asking you to "verify your M-Pesa account" with a link to a fake website that steals your PIN.

**How to protect yourself:**  
- Check the sender’s email address and URLs carefully  
- Never click suspicious links or attachments  
- Enable 2-Factor Authentication (2FA)  
- Report suspicious messages

### 2. Malware
**Explanation:**  
Malware = "malicious software". It includes viruses, trojans, spyware, and worms designed to damage, spy on, or take control of your device.

**Real-life example:**  
Downloading a "cracked" game that installs a keylogger to record everything you type.

**How to protect yourself:**  
- Install antivirus/anti-malware and keep it updated  
- Only download software from official sources  
- Keep OS and apps patched  
- Don’t open unknown email attachments

### 3. Ransomware
**Explanation:**  
Ransomware encrypts your files and demands payment, usually in crypto, to give you the decryption key.

**Real-life example:**  
The 2017 "WannaCry" attack that locked hospitals' computers worldwide and demanded Bitcoin to unlock patient records.

**How to protect yourself:**  
- Regularly backup important files to an external drive/cloud  
- Keep software updated  
- Use email filtering to block malicious attachments  
- Never pay the ransom — report to authorities

### 4. Social Engineering
**Explanation:**  
Manipulating people to give up confidential information by building trust, instead of hacking tech directly.

**Real-life example:**  
Someone calls pretending to be "IT Support" and asks for your password to "fix your computer".

**How to protect yourself:**  
- Verify identity before sharing info  
- Train yourself and staff on social engineering tactics  
- Follow company security policies  
- Be skeptical of urgent requests

### 5. Password Attacks
**Explanation:**  
Attempts to guess or crack passwords using methods like brute force, dictionary attacks, or credential stuffing.

**Real-life example:**  
Hackers using a list of leaked passwords from Facebook to try logging into Gmail accounts because people reuse passwords.

**How to protect yourself:**  
- Use strong, unique passwords + password manager  
- Enable 2FA everywhere  
- Don’t reuse passwords across sites  
- Lock accounts after multiple failed attempts

### 6. DoS / DDoS
**Explanation:**  
**DoS** = Denial of Service. **DDoS** = Distributed Denial of Service.  
Attackers flood a website/server with traffic to make it crash and unavailable to real users.

**Real-life example:**  
The 2016 DDoS attack on Dyn DNS that took down Netflix, Twitter, and Spotify for hours.

**How to protect yourself:**  
- Use firewalls and DDoS protection services like Cloudflare  
- Set traffic rate limits  
- Keep systems patched  
- Have a disaster recovery plan
