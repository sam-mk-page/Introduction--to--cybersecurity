##Understanding Common Cyber Threats

## Part A: Definitions

**1. What is a cyber threat?**  
A cyber threat is anything that can potentially harm computer systems, networks, or data. It’s basically any danger online — it could be a hacker, a virus, or even just weak security. It doesn’t have to attack yet, but the risk is there.

**2. What is a vulnerability?**  
A vulnerability is a weakness in a system. Like leaving your phone unlocked or using "1234" as a password. It’s the open door that hackers look for to get in.

**3. What is a cyber attack?**  
A cyber attack is when someone actually tries to cause harm. They use a threat and exploit a vulnerability to steal information, delete files, or shut down systems.

**4. Explain the difference between a threat, a vulnerability, and an attack.**  
Think of it like this:  
- **Threat** = The robber waiting outside  
- **Vulnerability** = The unlocked window  
- **Attack** = The robber climbing in through the window  
So the threat is the potential danger, the vulnerability is the weakness, and the attack is when the danger is used.

## Part B: Research - 5 Common Cyber Attacks

### 1. Phishing
**What it is:** Trick emails and messages that pretend to be from trusted companies.  
**How it works:** You get a text or email saying "Your M-PESA account will be blocked. Click here." The link goes to a fake site that steals your login and PIN.  
**Real-life example:** In Kenya many people have received fake "M-PESA reversal" messages asking them to send money back or enter details on a fake website.  
**How to protect:** Don’t click unknown links. Check the sender. Never share OTPs or PINs. Turn on 2-factor authentication.

### 2. Malware
**What it is:** Malicious software made to damage or spy on your device.  
**How it works:** You download a "free movie" or app and it secretly installs viruses or spyware that steals your data.  
**Real-life example:** The WannaCry attack in 2017 locked hospital computers around the world and demanded payment.  
**How to protect:** Use antivirus, keep your phone and computer updated, and only download from Play Store/App Store.

### 3. Ransomware
**What it is:** A virus that locks all your files and asks for money to unlock them.  
**How it works:** It encrypts your photos, documents, everything. Then a message pops up: "Pay $300 in Bitcoin to get your files back."  
**Real-life example:** The 2021 Colonial Pipeline attack in the US shut down fuel supply for days because of ransomware.  
**How to protect:** Backup your files regularly, don’t open weird email attachments, and keep security software updated.

### 4. Social Engineering
**What it is:** Hacking people, not computers.  
**How it works:** Someone calls pretending to be from the bank or Safaricom. They sound friendly and ask for your details "for verification."  
**Real-life example:** Fake calls claiming "This is Equity Bank, we noticed suspicious activity. Please confirm your card number."  
**How to protect:** Real banks never ask for PINs on call. Hang up and call the official customer care number yourself.

### 5. Denial-of-Service (DoS/DDoS)
**What it is:** Flooding a website with so much traffic that it crashes.  
**How it works:** Hackers use many computers to send millions of requests to one website at once until it can’t handle it.  
**Real-life example:** In 2016 an attack on Dyn took down Twitter, Netflix, and Spotify for several hours.  
**How to protect:** Companies use firewalls and DDoS protection services. As a user there’s not much you can do except wait.

## Part C: Critical Thinking

**1. Which cyber attack do you think is the most dangerous today, and why?**  
I believe **Ransomware** is the most dangerous today.  
Because it doesn’t just steal data, it shuts down entire organizations. Hospitals can’t access patient records, banks can’t process transactions, schools can’t operate. And people get desperate and pay. It causes real damage in the real world, not just online.

**2. If you were the IT manager of a bank, which three security measures would you implement first to protect customers?**  
1. **Train the staff and customers** - Most attacks start with someone clicking a phishing link. If people know what to look for, we stop 80% of problems.
2. **Enforce Multi-Factor Authentication** - Every login should need password + OTP. Even if a password leaks, the account is still safe.
3. **Daily backups and fast updates** - Backup all customer data every day and keep it offline. Also update all systems immediately when security patches come out so hackers can’t use old weaknesses.
